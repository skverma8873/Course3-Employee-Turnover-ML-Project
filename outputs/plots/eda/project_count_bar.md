# Analysis: Project Count Bar Chart

## What is this chart?
A grouped bar chart where employees are segmented by the number of projects they are assigned (2 through 7). For each group, two bars appear side by side:

- **Green bar** = employees who stayed
- **Orange bar** = employees who left

The taller the orange bar relative to the green, the higher the departure rate within that project-count group.

---

## Reading the Pattern

| Projects Assigned | Departure Pattern | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| **2 projects** | Orange bar >> Green bar | Under-utilised employees leave at very high rates. |
| **3–4 projects** | Green bar >> Orange bar | Sweet spot — most employees stay. Workload is balanced. |
| **5 projects** | Near-equal or slight green advantage | Getting heavy, but still mostly manageable. |
| **6–7 projects** | Orange bar rising sharply again | Overloaded employees are burning out and leaving. |

---

## The U-Shaped Departure Curve

The relationship between projects and turnover forms a **U-shape**:

- **Too few projects (2)** → Boredom, feeling sidelined, lack of career growth → Leaves
- **Right amount (3–4)** → Engaged, contributing, valued → Stays
- **Too many (6–7)** → Burnout, overwhelm, work-life breakdown → Leaves

This means Portobello Tech is losing employees from **both ends** of the workload spectrum simultaneously. The causes are completely different, but the outcome (resignation) is the same.

---

## Why Under-Utilised Employees Leave

An employee with only 2 projects may:
- Feel their skills are not being used
- Believe they are being overlooked for important work
- See limited advancement prospects
- Find the role monotonous compared to what they expected

These employees often leave quietly and are not flagged by performance-based early warning systems because they are not underperforming — they are underdeployed.

---

## Why Overloaded Employees Leave

An employee juggling 6–7 projects may:
- Be working 60+ hour weeks (confirmed by the hours distribution)
- Be experiencing physical and mental burnout
- Feel the company is exploiting their capability without fair recognition or compensation
- Have received no relief despite raising concerns

These employees often leave suddenly after a single triggering event (a denied promotion, a missed bonus, a family incident).

---

## HR Decision-Making Implications

1. **Flag employees with 2 projects** — schedule a growth and engagement conversation immediately. Offer new assignments, cross-functional projects, or mentoring roles.

2. **Protect the 3–4 project range** — this is the retention sweet spot. Avoid piling additional work onto employees already in this healthy range.

3. **Urgent workload audit for 6–7 project employees** — identify which projects can be redistributed. These employees are the most immediate flight risk from an operational standpoint.

4. **Do not use "number of projects" as a proxy for engagement without context** — a high-performing employee with 7 projects who is also highly satisfied is different from one who has 7 projects, low satisfaction, and no promotion in 3 years.
