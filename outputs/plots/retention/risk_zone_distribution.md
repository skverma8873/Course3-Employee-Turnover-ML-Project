# Analysis: Employee Risk Zone Distribution

## What is this chart?
A bar chart showing how the 3,000 test-set employees are distributed across the four risk zones, based on the turnover probability scores generated by the Random Forest model.

Each employee receives a score between 0% and 100% representing their estimated probability of leaving. These scores are then bucketed into four zones:

| Zone | Score Range | Colour |
|---|---|---|
| **Safe** | < 20% | Green |
| **Low-Risk** | 20% – 60% | Yellow |
| **Medium-Risk** | 60% – 90% | Orange |
| **High-Risk** | > 90% | Red |

---

## Results

| Zone | Employees | % of Test Set | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Safe** | **2,197** | **73.2%** | Model estimates < 20% chance of leaving |
| **Low-Risk** | **96** | **3.2%** | Model estimates 20–60% chance of leaving |
| **Medium-Risk** | **60** | **2.0%** | Model estimates 60–90% chance of leaving |
| **High-Risk** | **647** | **21.6%** | Model estimates > 90% chance of leaving |

**Total at-risk employees (Low + Medium + High): 803 (26.8%)**

---

## What Each Zone Means and What HR Should Do

### Safe Zone (Green) — 2,197 employees (73.2%)

These employees have a very low predicted probability of leaving. No urgent action is required, but these employees should not be ignored:
- Continue standard practices: performance reviews, recognition, learning opportunities.
- Maintain healthy workloads to keep them in this zone.
- Monitor for any individual who migrates toward Low-Risk in future model runs — early movement is the easiest to reverse.

---

### Low-Risk Zone (Yellow) — 96 employees (3.2%)

The model detects early-stage disengagement signals. These employees are showing some of the characteristics of eventual leavers, but not yet at a critical level.

**Recommended actions:**
- Stay interviews: "What would make you want to stay long-term?"
- Career path clarity and skill development programmes.
- Mentoring pairing with a senior leader.
- Regular manager check-ins (monthly rather than quarterly).

**Why act now:** Employees in this zone still have significant attachment to the company. Proactive engagement here is the most cost-effective retention intervention — much cheaper and easier than rescuing a High-Risk employee who has mentally already moved on.

---

### Medium-Risk Zone (Orange) — 60 employees (2.0%)

The model is detecting strong departure signals. These employees are actively considering leaving. Time is limited.

**Recommended actions:**
- Urgent 1:1 with direct manager within the week.
- Full workload and project-count review.
- Compensation benchmarking against current market rates.
- HR Business Partner escalation.
- Identify whether they belong to the "Burned-Out Star" or "About to be Poached" archetype and tailor the response accordingly (see clustering analysis).

**Why the urgency:** Employees at 60–90% probability have typically already started conversations with recruiters or exploring alternatives. The window for meaningful intervention is measured in weeks, not months.

---

### High-Risk Zone (Red) — 647 employees (21.6%)

The model assigns greater than 90% probability of departure. These employees have, in all likelihood, already made their mental decision to leave. The goal here shifts from prevention to **damage limitation and genuine understanding of root cause**.

**Recommended actions:**
- Senior leadership involvement — not just line manager.
- Personalised retention package exploration (compensation uplift, title change, role redesign, working arrangement flexibility, promotion).
- Weekly check-ins to maintain connection and gather intelligence.
- Exit risk briefing prepared for each individual: what would be the cost and operational impact of their departure?
- Root cause analysis: identify common themes across the 647 (department, manager, tenure band, salary band) to fix systemic issues.

---

## The Most Critical Insight: The Bimodal Distribution

The distribution is **not gradual**. Most employees are either Safe (73%) or High-Risk (22%), with very little in between (Low-Risk 3.2% + Medium-Risk 2.0% = only 5.2%).

This means employees are **not slowly drifting away** over many months — they are crossing from "fine" to "I'm leaving" rapidly, often triggered by a specific event:
- A missed promotion
- A disappointing performance review outcome
- A frustrating project assignment or management decision
- A competitor's offer arriving at the right moment
- A personal life event that changes their priorities

**Strategic implication:** Standard annual engagement surveys will always be too slow to catch this pattern. The company needs:
1. **Monthly model runs** to catch individuals as they first enter the Low-Risk zone (3.2%), before they escalate to High-Risk.
2. **Manager training** to recognise the early behavioural signals that precede a score change.
3. **Rapid response protocols** — when the model flags a High-Risk employee, there should be a defined escalation path that activates within 48 hours, not the next quarterly cycle.

---

## Business Impact Estimate

Assuming the full workforce is ~15,000 employees and ~21.6% are High-Risk:
- **~3,240 employees** may be at imminent risk of leaving across the full organisation.
- At an industry-standard replacement cost of 6 months' salary (~£25,000 average), the potential cost of inaction is approximately **£81 million**.
- Even retaining 20% of those High-Risk employees through targeted intervention would represent **~£16 million in avoided cost**.
