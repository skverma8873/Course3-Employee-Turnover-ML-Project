# Analysis: Classification Report — Gradient Boosting

## What is a Classification Report?
A classification report shows the quality of predictions broken down by outcome class (Stayed vs Left) across three metrics:

- **Precision:** Of all employees the model *predicted* for this class, what fraction was correct? High precision = few false alarms.
- **Recall:** Of all employees who *actually belong* to this class, what fraction did the model catch? High recall = few misses.
- **F1-Score:** The harmonic mean of precision and recall — balances both into a single score. Closer to 1.0 is better.

---

## Interpreting Each Number

**For the "Left" group (the critical one):**

**Precision = 0.922**
Of every 100 employees flagged as at-risk, 92 genuinely are. Only 8 are false alarms. This is a highly reliable signal for HR.

**Recall = 0.931**
Of every 100 employees who actually resigned, the model caught 93 of them in advance. About 7 slipped through undetected. This is strong performance.

**F1-Score = 0.927**
Well-balanced — neither precision nor recall is being sacrificed for the other. The overall combined score is excellent.

**For the "Stayed" group:**
Precision = 0.978, Recall = 0.976 — near-perfect identification of stayers. Very few unnecessary interventions for employees with no departure intent.

## Gradient Boosting vs Random Forest — Where the Gap Lies

| Metric | Gradient Boosting | Random Forest | Difference |
|---|---|---|---|
| Recall (Left) | 0.931 | 0.979 | RF catches 4.8% more real leavers |
| Precision (Left) | 0.922 | 0.980 | RF generates 5.8% fewer false alarms |
| F1 (Left) | 0.927 | 0.980 | RF is 5.3 points better overall |

In absolute terms on 3,000 test employees: Random Forest misses 15 leavers vs Gradient Boosting's 49. The 34-employee difference represents real people who would resign unexpectedly if Gradient Boosting were the production model instead of Random Forest.

---

## How Gradient Boosting Works (Plain English)

Gradient Boosting builds many small decision trees **sequentially**, where each new tree focuses on correcting the errors made by the previous one. This iterative error-correction process produces a highly accurate ensemble. It is slightly less accurate than Random Forest on this dataset, possibly because Random Forest's **parallel** ensemble of independent trees captures more diverse patterns simultaneously.

---

## HR Decision-Making Implications

- Gradient Boosting is an excellent model — suitable for production use if Random Forest were unavailable.
- Its Recall of 93.1% means HR would still catch the vast majority of real flight risks.
- The 0.922 Precision means HR alerts generated by this model are reliable — nearly 1 in 10 would be a false alarm, but that is an acceptable false alarm rate for a retention programme.
- Both Gradient Boosting and Random Forest are decisively better than Logistic Regression, confirming that non-linear, interaction-based models are required for this problem.
