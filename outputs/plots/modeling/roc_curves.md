# Analysis: ROC Curves

## What is this chart?
The ROC (Receiver Operating Characteristic) curve plots each model's ability to distinguish employees who will leave from those who will stay, across all possible decision thresholds.

- **X-axis** — False Positive Rate: how often the model incorrectly flags a stayer as a leaver
- **Y-axis** — True Positive Rate (Recall): how often the model correctly identifies a real leaver
- A curve that hugs the **top-left corner** is best (high recall, low false alarms)
- A **diagonal straight line** would represent random guessing (AUC = 0.50)
- **AUC (Area Under Curve)** summarises the entire curve as a single number: 0 to 1

---

## Results

| Model | AUC Score | Grade |
|---|---|---|
| **Random Forest** | **0.9957** | Near-perfect |
| **Gradient Boosting** | **0.9859** | Excellent |
| **Logistic Regression** | **0.8205** | Acceptable but clearly weaker |

---

## What AUC Means in Plain Terms

**AUC = 0.9957 (Random Forest):**
If you randomly pick one employee who left and one who stayed, the Random Forest model will correctly rank the leaver as higher risk **99.57% of the time**. It almost never gets this wrong.

**AUC = 0.9859 (Gradient Boosting):**
Gets the same comparison right 98.6% of the time. A very strong model — the gap from Random Forest is small.

**AUC = 0.8205 (Logistic Regression):**
Gets it right 82% of the time. Still well above random chance (50%), but noticeably weaker. In a dataset with ~24% departures, this gap translates to many more missed leavers and false alarms.

---

## Why is Logistic Regression Weaker?

Logistic Regression assumes a **linear** relationship between inputs and the outcome — meaning it expects each variable to contribute independently and proportionally. Employee turnover does not work that way.

A classic example from this dataset: an employee with **low satisfaction AND high evaluation AND 6+ projects** is dramatically more likely to leave than any one of those factors alone would suggest. This interaction effect (the cluster of burned-out stars) requires a model that can learn non-linear combinations of features.

**Random Forest** and **Gradient Boosting** are both decision-tree-based models. They split the data using branching "if this AND that" logic, which naturally captures these interaction effects.

---

## Which Model Was Selected?

**Random Forest** was selected as the production model based on highest AUC (0.9957).

This means all risk scores in the retention report are generated by the Random Forest model. The high AUC gives HR the confidence that the model's probability estimates are well-calibrated and reliable.

---

## HR Decision-Making Implications

- The AUC of 0.9957 means the system can be **trusted to rank employees by departure risk** accurately.
- When the model says an employee has a 95% probability of leaving, that score reflects a genuine signal — not noise.
- The ROC curve also shows the model performs consistently well across all thresholds, not just at one specific operating point. This means HR can choose their own intervention threshold (e.g., "flag anyone above 60%") without the model becoming unreliable.
